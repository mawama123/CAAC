FRAMING

Who is a child?

Art. 1 – Convention on the Rights of the Child

  • A child means every human being below the age of eighteen years, unless under the law applicable to the child, majority is attained earlier

FRAMING

Children are civilians — and more

The comprehensive POC agenda

  • Physical protection from the effects of hostilities
  • Safe, unimpeded humanitarian access & assistance
  • Prevention of forced displacement & return
  • Respect for IHL, human rights & refugee law
  • Accountability for violations and remedy

PLUS age-specific harms

The CAAC child-specific layer

  • Recruitment & use by armed forces/groups
  • Abduction, separation & unaccompanied minors
  • Conflict-related sexual violence (CRSV)
  • Attacks on schools, hospitals & protected objects
  • Denial of humanitarian access for children

Key point

CAAC is not a parallel humanitarian niche. It is a child-specific accountability and protection framework that operates inside and strengthens comprehensive POC.

Source: UN OCHA — Protection of Civilians, ICRC — IHL protection of children

Caution

These six categories anchor the Monitoring and Reporting Mechanism and trigger listing in the Secretary-General's annual report. They are accountability triggers but not a comprehensive list of the harms children suffer. Family separation, disability, trauma, and education loss are real harms that may fall outside the six.

Source: UN SG Report on CAAC (2025)

FRAMING

THE MONITORING AND REPORTING MECHANISM (MRM)

MRM

  • The MRM is put in place in a country once a party to a conflict in that country is listed in the annexes to the Secretary-General’s annual report on CAAC.

Country Task Force for

Monitoring and Reporting (CTFMR)

  • The Country Task Force for Monitoring and Reporting (CTFMR) is the organizational structure for the implementation of the MRM at the country level
  • Once the MRM is established all parties to conflict (state armed forces, armed groups, paramilitaries) are monitored.

Caution

The number of verified violations reported is just the tip of the iceberg. Many challenges like the access to affected ares to collect and verify information and systematic under-reporting remain.

Source: UN OCHA — Protection of Civilians, ICRC — IHL protection of children

EXERCISE

Applied scenario: triage under competing risks

The case

An armed group is recruiting boys (14–16) from the periphery of an IDP site; one commander has intermittently occupied a nearby school for storage. A siege has throttled humanitarian access for six weeks. A 15-year-old girl who escaped an armed group has requested help and fears retaliation. Your team has limited staff and no verified MRM data yet.

Your task (90 seconds to discuss)

  • Choose 2–3 priority actions and sequence them.
  • Name the grave violation(s) you would document via the MRM — and what you would NOT classify as one.
  • Identify one do-no-harm risk in your chosen response.

Debrief in speaker notes.

Source: Paris Principles on DDR of children

PROGRAMMING

Operational response and safeguards

Monitoring & accountability

  • MRM information management & verification standards
  • Safe, confidential data handling & survivor consent
  • Country task forces (CTFMRM)
  • Action plans & engagement for de-listing

Direct response services

  • Case management & referral pathways
  • Family tracing & reunification (FTR)
  • Release & reintegration (DDR for children)
  • Mental Health and Psychosocial Support (MHPSS)

Cross-cutting safeguards

  • Do-no-harm & conflict-sensitive analysis
  • Survivor-centred CRSV response
  • Risk-informed, disability-inclusive programming
  • Treat children associated with parties primarily as victims

Source: UN CAAC — Six Grave Violations / MRM, IASC — Guidelines on MHPSS

TAKEAWAYS & RESOURCES

Three things to carry forward

CAAC is a child-specific accountability and protection framework operating inside — not parallel to — comprehensive POC.

The six grave violations are MRM triggers and listing criteria, not the full universe of harm; keep monitoring and programming distinct but linked.

Sound CP action is risk-informed, survivor-centred, do-no-harm, and tied to civilian-harm mitigation, access, and accountability.
